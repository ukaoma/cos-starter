Intelligence is moving off the screen and into everyday life.

First it lived in apps. Then it moved onto our wrists, fingers, glasses and roads, and now it is learning to work in rooms. Each step asks a little less of your attention and does a little more with the world around you.

For nearly three centuries we built bodies with no mind inside.

In 1770 a machine sat down at a chessboard and beat half of Europe. The intelligence inside was a hidden human.

Wolfgang von Kempelen's Mechanical Turk toured the courts of Europe and America for eight decades and reportedly played Napoleon Bonaparte and Benjamin Franklin. It was a sensation because everyone wanted it to be true: a machine that perceives the world and acts in it. The body was convincing. The mind was a chess master crouched in the cabinet.[1]

That has been the shape of the failure ever since. Vaucanson's duck of 1739 flapped, ate and digested nothing.[2] Factory arms of the 1960s moved with superhuman precision and understood none of it. We could build bodies. We could not build the part that knows what the room means.

Then the order reversed. The last decade produced minds without bodies: models that read, reason, plan and write software, kept behind a text box. For the first time in the history of this dream, the missing piece is not the intelligence. It is the body, and the context to know whose world it is standing in.

The path ran from the screen to the world.

The story is not that hardware got smaller. Intelligence gained better senses, richer context and, at last, a way to act.

  1. 1739 to 1770

    The imitation

    • 1739

      Vaucanson's duck. It flapped, drank, ate and appeared to digest. The intelligence was projection.[2]

    • 1770

      The Mechanical Turk. Its brilliant machine mind was a human operator hidden in the cabinet.[1]

  2. 2004 to 2007

    The road becomes a problem you can measure

    • 2004

      No car finishes. Fifteen finalists entered DARPA's first Grand Challenge. None completed the desert route.[3]

    • 2005

      Five cars finish. Stanford won; five vehicles completed 132 autonomous desert miles. A failure became a field in eighteen months.[3]

    • 2007

      Traffic and intersections. Six vehicles finished the Urban Challenge among manned and unmanned traffic.[4]

  3. 2007 to 2015

    The computer moves onto the body

    • 2007

      iPhone. A full software platform in the hand, operated with our fingers.[5]

    • 2014 to 15

      Apple Watch. Timely information and health signals, built to be checked at a glance.[6]

    • 2015

      Oura Ring. The screen disappeared. It learns from sleep, temperature, heart rate and movement, then explains the pattern later.[7]

  4. 2020 to 2024

    The model learns to act

    • 2020

      Spot goes on sale. Boston Dynamics opened commercial sales after use in construction, energy and hazardous sites.[8]

    • 2023

      RT-2. A vision-language-action model trained on web and robot data emitted control instructions for a real robot.[9]

    • 2024

      GR00T, electric Atlas, π0. A humanoid foundation model, a commercial electric Atlas program and a generalist robot policy.[10][11][12]

  5. 2025 to 2026

    The product race

    • 2025

      Figure raises. More than $1 billion in Series C at a $39 billion post-money valuation, to scale Helix and manufacturing.[13]

    • 2025 to 26

      1X sells the home promise. NEO preorders at $20,000, with U.S. priority delivery stated for 2026.[14]

    • 2026

      Apptronik passes $935M. The question moves from whether a humanoid can walk to manufacturing and repeatable work.[15]

    • 2026

      The road got there first. Waymo reported 220.6 million rider-only miles through March 2026, by its own figures.[17]

Why now

A model that generalizes
Web knowledge and robot demonstrations now live in one model, so an action can be learned once and reused.[9][12]
A world it can rehearse in
Robots train in simulation and carry the policy into the machine, so practice outruns the calendar.[10]
A body built for work
Electric actuators, better batteries and dexterous hands turn prototypes into products for factories and homes.[11]

A day can become context, if you choose what it keeps.

The phone put compute in your pocket. Watches and rings made passive self-data normal. The next layer is a private record of the moments that matter, kept only with permission and only in places you can see.

Read the philosophyAI chief of staff vs a human one

Intelligence learns to work with us before it works around us.

Ambient systems practise the hard parts of physical life, timing, context, memory and restraint, without pretending today's hardware can do everything.

Now: beside you

It listens when invited, remembers the context you choose to give it, and surfaces the next useful thing while you stay the one acting.

  • Prepares you before a conversation
  • Keeps the key context in your line of sight
  • Captures commitments without breaking presence
  • Leaves judgment and action with you
Presence over interruption
It should help you stay in the room, not pull you into another screen.
Assistance before spectacle
A quiet, useful cue matters more than a cinematic demo.
Earned autonomy
The closer software gets to the physical world, the more reliability, consent and restraint matter.

Emerging: around you

It perceives a shared space, plans within real limits and acts through a car, an arm, a mobile platform or a humanoid body.

  • Navigates roads and rooms
  • Handles ordinary objects
  • Learns new physical tasks
  • Earns autonomy through bounded reliability

The honest near-term thesis is smaller and more useful: factories first, bounded tasks first, assistance before autonomy, and a long learning curve inside the home.
